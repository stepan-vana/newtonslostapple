# Velocity

Velocity is a physical vector quantity denoted by $v$, with the SI base unit m/s ($ \mathrm{ms^{-1}} $).

It describes how much the distance changes, or more precisely, what distance a body travels over a given time.  

- **Average velocity** is the total distance traveled divided by the total time (see formula below).
- **Instantaneous velocity** is the velocity at a specific moment in time.

> **velocity:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{v = \frac{s}{t}}
> $$

Velocity can also be expressed in other units, e.g. km/h ($ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}} $).

> **Conversion m/s → km/h:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{v_{m/s} \cdot 3.6 = v_{km/h}}
> $$

> **Conversion km/h → m/s:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{v_{km/h}}{3.6} = v_{m/s}}
> $$

From the formula for average velocity, time and distance can also be derived.

> **Distance:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{s = v \cdot t}
> $$

> **Time:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{t = \frac{s}{v}}
> $$

### Examples:

**1.)** A car traveled between two cities in 1.5 hours, and the distance between the cities is 80 km. What was its average velocity?

$$
v = \frac{80}{1.5}\,\mathrm{km/h} \approx \underline{53\,\mathrm{km/h}}
$$

**2.)** A ball moved across a table at a velocity of 0.5 m/s; the table is 2 m long. How long will it take for the ball to travel across the table? Ignore resistive forces.

$$
t = \frac{2}{0.5}\,\mathrm{s} = \underline{4\,\mathrm{s}}
$$

**3.)** John rode a bike for 2 hours at an average velocity of 10 km/h. What distance did he travel?

$$
s = 10 \cdot 2 = \underline{20\,\mathrm{km}}
$$
