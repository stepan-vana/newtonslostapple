In classical mechanics, **linear momentum** (commonly referred to simply as momentum) is a fundamental vector physical quantity that quantifies the quantity of motion of a moving body. Intuitively, it serves as an operational measure of how difficult it is to bring a moving object to a complete halt under the influence of an opposing force.

---

## Linear Momentum 

Momentum combines both the inertial property (mass) and the kinematic property (velocity) of a physical system into a single vector quantity. Consequently, a massive object moving slowly or a light object moving at a very high velocity can possess an identical magnitude of momentum.



### Mathematical Formulation

The linear momentum of a non-relativistic rigid body is defined as the product of its mass and its instantaneous velocity vector:

$$\boxed{\vec{p} = m\vec{v}}$$

* **$\vec{p}$** — Linear momentum vector. It shares the exact same directional vector as the velocity.
* **$m$** — Mass of the object ($\text{kg}$).
* **$\vec{v}$** — Velocity vector ($\text{m/s}$).

### Unit of Measurement

The SI derived unit for momentum is the **kilogram meter per second** ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ or $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$). It is dimensionally defined as:

$$[\vec{p}] = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1} = \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$$

The alternative unit Newton-second ($\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$) highlights the direct relationship between momentum and force, known as the *impulse* of a force.

---

## Law of Conservation of Momentum

The **Law of Conservation of Momentum** is a fundamental conservation principle in physics, derived directly from Newton's Third Law of Motion and associated with spatial homogeneity (translation invariance via Noether's theorem). 

It dictates that within an **isolated (closed) system**—a system completely free from external forces—the total vector momentum remains invariant over time, regardless of any internal interactions, collisions, or explosions occurring within the system boundaries.



### Discrete System Formulation

For an isolated system consisting of $n$ discrete interacting bodies, the vector sum of all individual momenta at any point in time equals a constant vector value:

$$\boxed{\vec{p}_1 + \vec{p}_2 + \dots + \vec{p}_n = \text{const.}}$$

This means that the total momentum before an interaction (such as an elastic or inelastic collision) is strictly equal to the total momentum after the interaction:

$$\vec{p}_{\text{total, before}} = \vec{p}_{\text{total, after}}$$

### Differential Formulation

Expressed in continuous time using calculus, the time derivative of the total momentum vector of an isolated system is zero, denoting zero net change:

$$\boxed{\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{p}}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0}$$
