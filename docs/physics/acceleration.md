# Acceleration

Acceleration is a physical quantity describing how velocity changes over time. It is denoted by $ a $ and has the SI unit $ \mathrm{m/s^2} $ ($ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}} $).  
**Important note:** Acceleration can also describe the change of **direction** of movement, more precisely acceleration describes the change of the **velocity vector**.

> **Acceleration:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{a = \frac{v}{t}}
> $$

To calculate the displacement in uniformly accelerated motion (i.e. $ a = \mathrm{const.} $), the formula $ s = vt $ cannot be used. Instead, we use the equation below.

> **Displacement in uniformly accelerated motion:**
>
> $$
> \boxed{s = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2}
> $$

where:

- $ v_0 $ ... initial velocity before the body starts accelerating
- $ t $ ... time interval for which we compute the displacement (as in $ s = vt $)
- $ a $ ... acceleration

This relation is derived from the area under the velocity function $ v(t) $:

$$
s = \int v(t)\,dt = \int (v_0 + at)\,dt = \boxed{v_0 t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2}
$$

It is also useful to combine the acceleration and displacement relations as follows:

$$
s = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{v}{t}\right)t^2 = \boxed{v_0 t + \frac{vt}{2}}
$$

---

## Free fall

Because free fall is a accelerated motion, we can use these equations. The gravitational acceleration is $g\approx 9,81 \frac{m}{s^2} \doteq 10\frac{m}{s^2}$.

### Example:

**1.)** A tree fell on Newtons head from height of 3 m. How long was the apple falling?  
Lets change the $s$ for $h$ like height.

$$
h=\frac{1}{2}at^2=\frac{1}{2}gt^2\implies 2h=gt^2\implies t^2=\frac{2h}{g}\implies t=\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot 3}{10}}s \implies \underline{t=0,775\space s}
$$
